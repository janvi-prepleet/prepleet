## Clarify the question

Estimate the number of cabs running on a typical day in the Mumbai metropolitan region. Include traditional taxis and app cabs. The figures below are illustrative interview assumptions, not measured transport statistics.

## Structure

**Cabs = (Population × daily rider share × trips per rider) ÷ trips per cab per day**

## Assumptions

| Input                 | Assumption | Reason                             |
| --------------------- | ---------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Population            | 20 million | Rounded metropolitan population    |
| Daily cab riders      | 4%         | Most commuters use other transport |
| Trips per rider       | 1.5        | Some riders take a return trip     |
| Trips per cab per day | 12         | A rough daily operating capacity   |

## Calculation

1. 20 million × 4% = 800,000 daily riders.
2. 800,000 × 1.5 = 1.2 million daily trips.
3. 1.2 million ÷ 12 = **100,000 active cabs**.

## Sanity check

The estimate is approximately one cab for every 200 residents. Discuss whether this fits a dense city with a large rail network. Then test your most influential assumption: what happens if the daily rider share is 3% instead of 4%?
